LTAs Gain Traction; PT to $1,625 with EPS 
Remaining Well >$100
Summary
With LTAs now firmly in place across most of the industry, we are again raising C2027-
2029 estimates and expect EPS to remain comfortably >$100 throughout the period, 
with MU generating over $400B in FCF across the same timeframe. We believe the 
market will start to put a more “normal” multiple on the stock and  MU will continue to 
re-rate higher as more details emerge about the structural changes AI has driven to the 
entire memory complex. Our supply chain work on Long Term Agreements (LTAs) across 
the memory industry suggests that up to 30% of DDR volumes industry-wide will be 
soon locked in at pricing that is just slightly below current levels and these agreements 
will allow MU to trade some near-term revenue for demand visibility and a smoother 
earnings profile. Consequently, we are raising EPS across C27/28/29E to $155/$167/
$117 (vs. $133/$122/$77 prior) - and considering that investors typically reward stocks 
for durability and visibility, we see MU’s EPS remaining >$100 through C2029E as 
testament to the sort of lasting, structural change that should support a shift toward a 
broader semi multiple. Net, we lift our PT from $535 to $1,625, now based on ~15x 
NTM P/E (vs. prior SoTP) on C2029E EPS of $117 (1-yr discounted), and reiterate Buy.
 Long Term Agreements (LTAs) - Details and Rationale
In contrast to prior periods where offtake agreements were simply volume based, these 
new ‘enhanced’ LTAs now incorporate longer durations, fixed volume commitments and 
- most importantly - a partially fixed pricing framework. It is our understanding that 
these 'enhanced' LTAs now span over a ~3-5 year period in either a '2+3' structure (two 
years of fixed terms, three years variable), or a '3+2' format. In DRAM, we think 20-30% 
of industry C2027 DDR bit shipments will now be covered under 'enhanced' LTAs. By 
company, we assume 18% SK Hynix, 20% MU, and 30% Samsung – this is contracted 
volume with fixed pricing, while pricing will still ‘float’ for the remainder of bits. At this 
point, we  believe hyperscalers alone to have currently secured ~60-70% of the industry 
Server DDR5 volumes under 'enhanced' LTAs - in other words, guaranteeing MU and 
others offtake for this volume. In NAND, a similar pricing structure may be emerging, 
with roughly 20% of volumes potentially moving to fixed-volume agreements but 
Samsung, SK Hynix, and likely MU seem less willing to commit new cleanroom capacity 
to NAND with the same level of certainty as DRAM. In assessing the rationale for 
'enhanced' LTAs, we see two primary benefits for memory suppliers: (1) smoother 
earnings/revenue profile and higher cross-cycle ROIC, and (2) improved visibility into 
now-committed customer demand. On cyclicality, incorporating LTAs into our MU 
model suggests these agreements should reduce the magnitude of DDR peak-to-trough 
pricing swings by roughly 2x compared to a scenario with no committed volumes or 
pricing structure. Even more important, this scenario yields MU EPS of >$100 even out in

Summary of Model Changes
We now expect the DRAM industry to remain undersupplied until at least C2Q28 (vs. 
4Q27 prior), and NAND undersupply to last until 4Q27 (was 3Q27). An additional driver 
of upside vs. our prior model stems from higher HBM ASP ($/GB) assumptions - 
something we highlighted already early in April - as MU/SK/Samsung intend to rebuild a 
premium for HBM pricing into C2027, leading us to now model MU HBM ASP up 
~50% Y/Y (vs. +35% Y/Y prior) on unchanged MU HBM bit shipment assumptions of 
7.78B Gb for C2026 and 12.05B Gb for C2027. Into CQ3:26 our industry model now 
also considers the impact of LTAs - as primarily reflected in the magnitude of the 
quarterly DDR (up ~8% Q/Q) and NAND (up ~9% Q/Q) industry contract pricing 
increase. This implies Q/Q pricing growth now normalizing back toward the low SD% 
range after  three, consecutive quarters of pricing increases averaging >50%. 
Valuation: PT to $1,625 (from $535)
We raise our price target from $535 to $1,625 based on a ~15x NTM P/E multiple 
(noting we see no reason why MU should trade a whole lot differently than NVDA in 
terms of P/E), which is in line with the 3-yr average, and applied to our ~$117 C2029E 
EPS and discounted back to C2028E using a COE of ~12%. Importantly, we anchor on 
C2029E EPS as we believe it best reflects MU’s through-cycle earnings power under LTAs 
because, by that point, our model assumes a moderate memory downcycle, albeit with 
LTAs buoying the company’s earnings base.

ValuatioN
We raise our price target from $535 to $1,625. In doing so, we shift our valuation 
framework to a NTM P/E methodology from a prior SoTP approach. We believe this 
change is warranted as Long Term Agreements (LTAs) are fundamentally changing the 
earnings profile of Micron, driving a structural re-rating of the shares. Specifically, while 
MU has historically traded as a hyper-cyclical name, we now see LTAs effectively 
anchoring demand and pricing, which should materially reduce earnings volatility and 
enhance visibility. As a result, we believe MU increasingly merits a valuation in line with 
broader Semiconductor peers, rather than at a discounted, hyper cycle-driven multiple. 
Net, our price target is based on a ~15x NTM P/E multiple (vs. our prior ~5x NTM P/E 
multiple implied by our SoTP valuation), applied to our ~$117 C2029E EPS and 
discounted back to C2028E using a COE of ~11.8%. Importantly, we anchor on C2029E 
EPS as we believe it best reflects MU’s through-cycle earnings power under LTAs as,  by 
that point, our model assumes a moderate memory downcycle, albeit with LTAs buoying 
the company’s earnings base.

Company Description
Micron Technology Inc. designs and manufactures semiconductor memory and storage 
solutions. Micron operates in four segments. The Compute and Networking Business Unit 
serves the client, cloud server, enterprise, graphics, and networking markets. The Mobile 
Business Unit addresses the needs of  the mobile handset and tablet markets. The Storage 
business unit sells into cloud, enterprise and client storage markets. The Embedded Business 
Unit sells memory to automotive, industrial, and consumer customers. Micron was founded in 
1978 and is headquartered in Boise, ID.
Valuation Method and Risk Statement
Micron Technology Inc (MU): to arrive at our PT we use a valuation method solely based on 
NTM P/E. Memory has been fairly correlated to global GDP. Therefore from a macro 
perspective there could be potential downside to our PT should economic conditions worsen 
and leading to demand destruction. Should ASP declines be more severe than our 
expectations, there could be more downside in the stock. Scaling both DRAM and NAND is 
highly complex and risks on the Technology front abound. There could be further downside 
should technology migration be slower than we expect. On the upside, while we expect ASPs 
to decline on account of incremental supply addition, any delays to the timelines could 
moderate the growth of supply and create an upward bias to ASPs and MU. Other risks 
include a incremental reliance of AI on memory driving up content in Data Center servers and 
devices (PCs, SPs, etc).
